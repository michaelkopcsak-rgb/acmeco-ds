## Overview

Label / value rows: AcmeCo's property facts ("Building Size 22,400 SF"). The audit found seven designs for this one row on property pages (F21); this is one. It closes the property page's Property Facts gap and fills the space cards on phones (decision 47).

## When to use

- For facts about one thing, each a short label and a short value: a property's size, type and year, a space's size, rate and use.
- `columns={2}` for a longer list in a wide column: two facts on a row from 560px, one below that, as on AcmeCo's phone.

## When not to use

- Not to compare several things fact by fact (all the spaces in a building): that's a table.
- Not for long text; a value is a word, a number or a short phrase.
- Not for form fields: those are TextField, Select and the rest.

## Accessibility

- A description list (`dl`): screen readers announce it as a list and read each label with its value.
- Labels are the secondary grey (#63666a, 5.77:1 on white, 5.20:1 on the grey band) and values ink (17.04:1), both 18px; the colour sets the label apart, not a thin weight.
- The rows follow reading order, so two columns read left to right, then down.

## Content

- Use AcmeCo's labels: "Total Space", "Property Type", "Year Built", "Rental Rate", "Space Use", "Availability".
- Write units out as AcmeCo does ("22,400 SF", "$24.50 /SF/YR").

## Evidence

- OBSERVED (retail property page, 1700px and 400px): 20/24 text, black labels at weight 100 and values at 400, the label taking 45% of its pair (282 of 628px; 153 of 340px on the phone), 8px after the label and under each row, two pairs a row on desktop and one on the phone.
- Now: 18/27 (the nearest size on the scale, decision 9), weight 400 for both (decision 4) with grey labels, 24px between pairs (PROPOSED: AcmeCo's pairs touch).
